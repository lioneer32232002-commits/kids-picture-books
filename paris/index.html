Hello, Paris!

Paris is one of the most famous cities in the world. It has wide streets, old bridges, a giant iron tower and a cathedral full of stone creatures. But Paris wasn't always big. Long, long ago, it was just a tiny village on an island.

This is Lea, who lives in Paris, and her friend Gus, a little stone gargoyle. Gus has been watching Paris grow for a very long time. Let's travel back in time with them!

👆 Tap the glowing circles. Tap 📖 Read to me to hear the story. Press Next ▶ to keep reading, or jump straight to the game.

More than 2,000 years ago, a group of people called the Parisii lived by a big river, the Seine. They fished, traded, and sailed boats up and down the water. Later, the Romans came and built a town there called Lutetia.

Paris got its name from the Parisii! And the river made Paris busy and rich. That's why there's a boat on Paris's flag even today. 👆 Tap the shield.

⛵ Paris's motto is in Latin: "Fluctuat nec mergitur." It means "Tossed by the waves, but never sinks." Like a brave little boat!

In 1163, the people of Paris started building a giant cathedral called Notre-Dame. There were no trucks and no electric cranes. Workers carved every stone by hand and lifted them with wooden wheels. It took almost 200 years to finish!

The roof is so heavy that it pushes the tall walls outward. So the builders added stone arms outside, called flying buttresses. They push back and hold the walls up. 👆 Tap the builders' tricks.

🐉 Gargoyles are really rain spouts! Rainwater runs through a little channel on their backs and shoots out of their mouths, far away from the walls, so the stones stay dry.

Around 1190, a king built a strong castle by the river to guard Paris. It was called the Louvre. Later kings turned it into a huge, fancy palace.

Then, in 1793, the palace became a museum for everyone. Now it's one of the biggest art museums in the world! 👆 Tap Long ago and Today.

🔺 The glass pyramid is the museum's front door. When it was built in 1989, lots of people said it looked strange. Today, it's one of the most famous sights in Paris!

Long ago, France had kings and queens who made all the rules. In 1789, many people in Paris were hungry, and they felt nobody was listening to them. On July 14, a big crowd took over an old castle-prison called the Bastille. Big changes began.

France chose three big ideas: Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. That means being free, being fair to everyone, and caring for each other. People in Paris still speak up when they think something isn't fair. The best tools are words, listening and voting. 👆 Tap the sky!

🎆 Every July 14 is France's big birthday party, with a parade and fireworks over the Eiffel Tower. The Bastille is gone now. Some of its stones were used to build a bridge!

About 170 years ago, old Paris was crowded and dark. The streets were tiny and twisty, and dirty water ran right down the middle. So from 1853 to 1870, a man named Haussmann gave Paris a giant makeover.

Workers built long, wide streets called boulevards, parks, and rows of creamy stone buildings. And under the streets, they built huge sewer tunnels to carry dirty water away. 👆 Peek underground!

🦨 Paris's sewers are so big that people once took boat tours through them! The tunnels under Paris are longer than the road from Paris to Rome.

In 1889, Paris threw a giant world's fair. For it, Gustave Eiffel's company built the tallest tower in the world, out of 18,038 iron pieces and about 2.5 million rivets! Many artists said it was ugly, and it was only supposed to stay for 20 years.

But the tower was great for sending radio messages, so it was saved. Now people love it! On a hot, sunny day, the iron on the sunny side stretches a tiny bit, and the top leans away from the sun. 👆 Slide the sun.

🎨 Every 7 years, painters give the tower a new coat, about 60 tonnes of paint. That's as heavy as 10 big elephants!

In 1900, Paris opened its first underground train, the Métro. Now trains zoom through tunnels under the whole city, and almost nobody in Paris lives far from a station.

Look at the entrance! An architect named Hector Guimard made it out of green iron that curls like plants and flowers. 👆 Tap the lamps, and listen for the train!

🌱 Guimard's plant-like style is called Art Nouveau, which means "new art." You can still walk through about 86 of his entrances in Paris today.
🔨 Rebuild Notre-Dame
Tap to help!

On April 15, 2019, a fire burned Notre-Dame's old wooden roof, and its tall spire fell. People all over the world felt very sad. But the people of Paris said: "We will build it again."

Carpenters chose big oak trees and shaped the beams by hand with axes, the way builders did 800 years ago. Stonemasons, glassmakers and painters helped too. In December 2024, Notre-Dame opened again, with a new spire and a golden rooster on top! 👆 Help rebuild!

🐓 The new golden rooster on the spire has flame-shaped feathers. Its designer calls it a phoenix, a bird from old stories that rises again after a fire.

You Know Paris!

A tiny island village became a giant city. People built a cathedral, a castle that became a museum, wide boulevards, an iron tower and trains under the ground. And when something broke, they built it again.
