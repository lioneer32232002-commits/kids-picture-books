The Bridge in 435 Pieces

This is Hao, and this is his grandma, A-ma. When A-ma was a little girl, she walked across a beautiful bridge in Taipei every week.

It was called the Meiji Bridge. But today, you can't walk on it. It lies in 435 pieces! What happened?

The first bridge here was made of iron, in 1901. Later the river was changed, and a new bridge was built. It opened in 1933, more than 90 years ago!

The new bridge had graceful arches, a stone railing, and two pairs of shiny bronze lamps. People loved to walk across it. 👆 Tap around.

🌉 The bridge led from the city to a hill by the river, where today's Grand Hotel stands. It was about 17 m wide, wider than most streets back then.
How does an arch stand up?

An arch is made of stones shaped like wedges. Builders put them on a wooden frame, starting from the bottom on both sides.

The last stone goes at the very top: the keystone. Now the stones push on each other and hold each other up, and the frame can come away! 👆 Build an arch, or try taking the frame away too early.

🏛️ People have built arches for thousands of years. The bridge's arches were made of concrete with steel inside, but they work the same way.
The bridge's long life

Over many years, the bridge changed. After 1945 it got a new name, the Zhongshan Bridge. More and more cars came, so it was made wider, and the bronze lamps were taken off.

Then, in 2002, something big happened. 👆 Travel through the years.

🚗 In 1968 the bridge was widened from 17 m to 23 m, so more cars could fit. That's when the lamps and the stone railing came off.

In 2001, a big storm called Typhoon Nari brought huge amounts of rain to Taipei, and many places flooded.

Here, the river is narrow. People worried that in a big flood, the old bridge's low arches and thick piers would get in the way of the water. 👆 Tap around the storm.

🌊 A bridge must let floodwater pass underneath. If the water can't get through, it backs up and rises even higher.
Build a bridge the flood can pass

So where should a bridge be? High enough that floodwater flows under it. But not too high, or the ramps get too steep for cars.

And not too many piers in the water: each one pushes the flood a little higher. 👆 Try it, then send a flood!

📏 Engineers look at old floods to see how high the water can get, then leave a little extra space under the bridge, just in case.

So in December 2002, workers began taking the old bridge apart. The city promised to keep it and build it again somewhere else.

They cut it into big blocks. Each block got a number, so one day it could be put back in the right place. 👆 Tap around.

🧩 In all, the bridge became 435 pieces. That's like a giant puzzle, with pieces as heavy as cars!
👆 Tap stone number 1

Why the numbers? Every piece of the bridge is a little different. A piece only fits in its own place, like a puzzle piece.

With the numbers, builders know exactly where each piece goes. 👆 Tap the stones in order, 1, 2, 3..., and watch the arch come back!

📸 Builders also took photos and made drawings before taking it apart. Numbers, photos and drawings together are like the picture on a puzzle box.

The pieces were taken to an old, empty swimming pool, and there they lie. Grass grows between them.

It has been more than 20 years. Why so long? Finding the right place is hard: it needs lots of space, it shouldn't block a river, and putting the pieces back together is tricky. 👆 Tap around.

🏷️ In 2004 the bridge became an official historic building, even in pieces. Over the years, the city looked at 16 places for it, but none was quite right.
If it were up to you, where would it go?

Now people in Taipei are talking about it again. Should the bridge be put back together? Where?

Every idea has good parts and hard parts. There isn't one right answer: people share ideas, listen to each other, and then decide together. 👆 Look at each idea.

🗳️ When a city decides something big, many people help: experts, students, neighbors, and the people everyone votes for.

In 2026, the city invited experts, university students and neighbors to look at the pieces and share ideas. Hao brought his drawing too!

The city plans to finish a report about how the bridge could be put back together in January 2027. Maybe, one day, A-ma and Hao will walk across it together. 👆 Tap around.

How to play Bridge Puzzle!

  1. 🧱 Arch: drag each numbered stone to the slot with the same number (or tap the stone, then the slot). Build up from both sides; the keystone goes last. Then take the frame away!
  2. 🔍 Find: tap the pieces in the pool in order. Tap a tarp to lift it.
  3. 🌊 Flood: pick the bridge's height and its piers, then press Test. The water must pass under it, and cars must get up the ramps.

You Know the Meiji Bridge!

Taipei's Meiji Bridge opened in 1933 with graceful arches and bronze lamps. In 2002 it was taken apart into 435 numbered pieces to help the river during floods. Today people are working out how and where to put it back together.
